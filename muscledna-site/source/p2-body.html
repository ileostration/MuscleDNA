        02MuscleHow muscle is built and how it is lost

        What muscle is

        Skeletal muscle is the tissue you train, and in adults it makes up roughly 30 to 40% of body mass (Janssen et al., 2000). Each fiber is one long cell that shortens when a nerve tells it to. Cardiac and smooth muscle work without conscious control.

        Select a muscleIt lights up on the figure.
        • Muscle fiber10 to 100 µm wideone cell with many nuclei
        • Myofibrilabout 1 µm widea chain of sarcomeres, end to end

        Inside a fiber

        A sarcomere is a stack of thin actin filaments and thick myosin filaments between two Z-discs. When calcium arrives, myosin heads grab actin and pull. Neither filament shortens. The Z-discs move closer together.

        How muscle builds

        Growth starts with a stimulus. Three signals are most often credited with turning hard training into bigger fibers (Schoenfeld, 2010).

        TensionHeavy or near-failure sets through a full range of motion.
        Metabolic stressThe burn and pump from higher reps and shorter rests.
        Muscle damageSmall tears from unfamiliar or stretched loading. Useful in small doses.

        After a hard session, muscle protein synthesis rises and stays elevated for roughly a day or two. Food and rest decide what that window builds. Satellite cells then donate extra nuclei, so a larger fiber can keep up its protein production.

        How muscle is lost

        Muscle shrinks when breakdown outpaces rebuilding for long enough. The usual causes are disuse, too little energy or protein, and age without resistance training. Strength drops faster than size does (Mitchell et al., 2012).

          05ResearchTraining volume and frequency

          More hard sets, more growth

          A meta-analysis found a graded dose-response between weekly sets and muscle growth. Each extra weekly set per muscle was linked to about 0.37% more growth (Schoenfeld et al., 2017).

          Frequency is a scheduling choice

          When weekly volume is matched, training a muscle more often does not meaningfully change growth. The authors note that frequency can be chosen by personal preference (Schoenfeld et al., 2019).

          Same weekly total, same expected growth. Spread the sets so every one is a hard one.

          What it means for your training
          • Count hard sets, not exercises. Weekly sets per muscle is the number that tracks growth.
          • Add volume gradually, and pull back when recovery slips.
          • Two sessions per muscle is a practical default. Pick a split you can repeat for months.
