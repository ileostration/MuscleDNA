# Build prompt: MuscleDNA (bodybuilding education, training plan and partner site), v18

Build a single-page website called "MuscleDNA" that teaches bodybuilding: how muscle works, how it is built and lost, how food drives growth, retention and fat loss, then presents the MuscleDNA training plan, volume and frequency research, partner brands, an about-us page, quotes from Mr. Olympia winners, a free research-guide PDF and a contact form. Voice: a professional bodybuilder and content specialist writing to the reader (second person, no meta text, no prompt-like messages in the page). Facts must be real and cited; anything that is not verified or not wired is labelled as such on the page. Deliver one self-contained index.html that runs with no build step (published as an artifact with the `downloads` capability for the PDF).

## Experience and motion (match igloo.inc's feel)
The page never scrolls. One fixed full-screen WebGL particle canvas sits behind ten fixed stages. Wheel, touch drag and keys are intercepted (no native scroll, no menu). There is NO snapping and NO stage-to-stage tween: wheel pixels (about 620 per stage), touch drag (half a screen per stage, with a little release inertia) and keys only move a continuous target (0 to 9). Two stages of exponential smoothing (rates about 8.5 and 5.5 per second, frame-rate independent) carry the figure to it, so the morph follows the hand and the user can stop anywhere inside a transition. Arrow keys, Space and PageUp/Down step to the next whole stage; Home/End jump. Inside a panel that can scroll, the wheel scrolls the panel first. SCROLL PLATEAU: each stage has a scroll-distance plateau at 100% (0.5 of a stage of wheel/touch travel); the mapping from raw scroll to stage position is flat across a plateau and eases between plateaus. The raw position is clamped to one stage around the stage on screen so a flick steps stage by stage. SNAP TO 100%: a card that is partly shown (a stage is clickable once more than 15% in) returns its stage to 100% the moment the user presses, focuses or types into any control on it (button, link, input, select, textarea, label), if that stage is the nearest one (within 0.6 of a stage). A small HUD shows the wordmark and "01 / 10 MuscleDNA".

## Particle story: nine poses, then DNA
- Inputs: nine 2:3 portrait pose renders plus the glowing DNA photo (16:9) as the last stage. Per pose, embed a 512x768 JPEG, a 320x480 binary body cut-out mask and 15 hand-placed body points (head, neck, shoulders, elbows, hands, navel, hips, knees, feet, in image coordinates).
- No intro animation and no wave: the screen is plain dark until the images are ready, then the DNA particles fade in (about 1.2 s) and the first card spins in. ORDER: DNA first as the hero (card 01), then poses 1 to 9 (cards 02 to 10). LOOP: scrolling past pose 9 comes back to the DNA and the other way round; the scroll position is an unbounded number reduced modulo 10. The HUD numbers start at 01 on the DNA hero.
- Sampling per pose (N = 160k desktop, 100k small screens): 64% BODY particles from inside the mask (80% on small screens), the rest BACKDROP particles from outside it. Body density ~ (0.3 + (luminance x local contrast)^0.8). POSITIONS come from the original render; COLOURS from a separate per-pose colour map. Backdrop density ~ L^1.3. On desktop spend most of the budget on the visible band head to just below the navel. Keep z relief tiny (0.008 of image height).
- SHARPNESS: the figures must read as high-definition, not blurry. Each particle's size follows the local sampling density (size ~ 1/sqrt(density), clamped to 0.5 to 1 of the base) so dense areas get small dots and sparse areas never leave big blobs; base point size is 1.25 x sqrt(imageHeightPx^2 x AR / N) on desktop (not 1.8); the fragment edge is a tight smoothstep (0.5 to 0.36) so each dot is crisp; colour gain is compensated for the smaller dots so the overall brightness equals the screenshot's. Backdrop dots have almost no size variation.
- Tracking: each particle keeps one identity through all poses. Pose i to i+1: warp the body particles with a similarity moving-least-squares map (Schaefer) from the 15 points of pose i to those of pose i+1, then pair with the next pose's samples by a balanced median-split match (a true bijection). Backdrop particles are paired the same way without a warp. Store the per-particle residual. The DNA target is paired with the last pose the same way.
- GPU morph for pose pairs: keypoints are uniforms. For mix t: body position = MLS(from; points lerp(A,B,t)) + residual x t', so a hand stays on a hand while the pose changes; backdrop and non-pose pairs use plain from/to mixing with a turbulence arc. Pose pairs use very little turbulence and almost no size swell.
- Framing (exact, from the user's reference screenshots). Desktop: every pose is registered to its 1920x1080 reference screenshot offline and stored as [image-centre x, y, image height in reference px, rotation deg]. At runtime scale the whole composition to the window WIDTH (c = max(W/1920, 0.7 x H/1080)); tall windows stop zooming out at 0.7 of the height-fit scale; the head keeps the reference's relative height. Exception, stage 7 (the lunge, About us): its feet are placed at 100% of the window height (feet y = H + (image centre y - feet y) x c, feet = lower of keypoints 13/14), so the feet touch the bottom of the screen on desktop; the phone framing is unchanged. Figure sides: poses 1 to 3 right, left, right; the rest as in the screenshots; the text card sits opposite the figure. RESPONSIVE (no jumps): two framings are blended by w = 1 - smoothstep(480, 1100, width). Width <= 480 (phones): the WHOLE figure, image height 64% of the screen, centred at the top, with the text card below it. Small screens use a contrast-enhanced colour map and a slightly larger point size.
- DNA (stage 1): only the helix rotates; colours graded to the reference; on desktop the whole 16:9 frame covers the screen.
- Muscle highlight on stage 2 (traps, delts, biceps, forearms, chest, abs, quads, calves): see Muscle highlight below.
- Colour: the reference screenshots show deep indigo bodies with magenta (left) and blue/cyan (right) rim light on a flat near-black background (6,8,14). One colour map per pose; a particle takes the colour at its (u, v) times a global gain times 1/(relative local density) so overlapping particles add up to the screenshot. Do not add a glow. The page background is the flat clear colour #06080e.
- Backdrop (near invisible, cursor-reactive): backdrop particles carry only a faint blue tint so the page reads as flat dark; they exist so that the cursor can light them. They never take the heat colours.

## Muscle highlight (stage 2)
Choosing a muscle on stage 2 (chips) recolours the figure on every pose and the colour stays identical across poses because particles keep their identity. Colours: the unselected body becomes dark purple (about 0.24, 0.05, 0.40 times the particle's own brightness); the selected muscle is a red gradient (deep red 0.40, 0.02, 0.10 at its edge to bright red 0.86, 0.09, 0.09 at its centre, edge fade over the outer 45% of the ellipse) with RED as the highlight. No white, no blur, no values above 1. The muscle keeps its DEFINITION (grooves and separations stay dark): the red is multiplied by the particle's photographic lightness relative to the median of the region (abs show as blocks, not a blob). Only the FRONT PLANE is coloured: particles whose lightness is below about 0.6 of the region median (shadow, grooves, side faces) stay purple. LEAVING THE STAGE RESETS the selection: chips unpressed, default info text, no highlight is retained when the stage is next entered.

## Cursor (igloo.inc "mouseFrost" ripple, small point of contact)
Run a small CPU simulation (about 160 cells tall, aspect-matched width) at 66 Hz: pointer velocity logic: e = distance moved per step, reset if idle > 0.15 s or jump > 0.3; targetVel += e*6, *= .88, clamp 0..1; vel lerps (0.1) toward 1-(1-targetVel)^4. Point of contact: splat a line from the previous to the current pointer position with a radius about 2x the cursor's own radius (11 px at rest to 26 px at speed, CSS px, multiplied by the figure-scale ratio on small screens, clamped 0.3 to 1.5, so the interaction stays proportional to the figure on small screens), falloff (1-d/r)^2. Ripple trail: every other step each cell takes the brightest of its 4 neighbours x 0.95; every step the field is multiplied by 0.970 and clamped to 1. Upload value and rim as an RGBA texture; in the vertex shader sample it at each particle's screen position and push particles along the gradient, scatter them, enlarge them and tint them icy blue-white. BACKDROP SOFTNESS: for backdrop particles the displacement is halved, the glow is capped at 0.8 and halved again (colour 50% less intense), the size boost is half the body's and there is no size difference between touched and untouched backdrop dots; the way the interaction ends (smooth decay) is how it feels throughout. HEAT (only on body particles, only while the cursor is ON the figure; silhouette lookup of 160x240): on contact the moving cursor emits dark red, 0.25 s later orange, then yellow, then white, then stops; each emission is stored per cell and copied outwards with the ripple; 0.5 s after emission it fades into the ripple colour. The episode ends when the cursor leaves the figure (0.12 s grace) or rests (0.35 s). Touched particles are drawn at the point size of the earlier build (factor .36 desktop, .36 x sqrt(N/160000)/mobile-size phones) so the touched dot size is the same proportion on every screen.

## Cards: one shared HUD-style frame
Every stage card is a game-UI frame: a chamfered double-outline (outer stroke plus an inner stroke at 6.5 px inset, corner radius about 10, glass fill clipped to the same shape) with a nook (about 18 px deep, 14 px on phones) in the top centre, bottom centre and mid-side. Colours stay the site's blue-grey. Paged cards put the PREVIOUS and NEXT arrows inside the left and right side nooks (centred in the nook, not inside the content) and the page number "n / total" inside the bottom nook. Until the user first presses an arrow on a visit to that stage the arrows nudge; after the first press they stop; the hint returns when the stage fully leaves. Disabled arrows dim at the first and last page. The frame is rebuilt on resize.
- PADDING: top and bottom padding equal left and right (32 px desktop, 26 px phone, 20 px mini cards, never below 20 px). The card height follows the active page's content: no empty space at the bottom, no overshoot when scrolling.
- SCROLL CUE: when a page is taller than the room, the card scrolls inside and a 1 px line crosses the card at the bottom of the scroll area, before the bottom padding. The scroll area height is chosen so the line passes through the vertical middle of a full-width line of text; scroll stops snap so the line sits at a line middle every time. The line is hidden at the end of the scroll, where nothing is cut.
- MINI CARDS: a large infographic gets its own small card below the main card, shown only on the page it belongs to and hidden on every other page. Mini cards: Muscle p2 (sarcomere graphic, slider and Fire), p3 (protein-synthesis chart), p4 (loss bars), p5 (grow-or-shrink widget and verdict); Nutrition p2 (macros and rings); Training p2 (hard sets per muscle, two columns). Small graphics (volume bars, the frequency graphic, stat rows) stay inside the main card. Two cards on one stage never count as one scroll area: each scrolls on its own.

## Tech and quality
three.js r128 (cdnjs, pinned); one THREE.Points, 160k particles (100k small screens), DPR capped at 2; additive blending. Respect prefers-reduced-motion (no spin or flip, no turbulence, no trail, no smoothing lag, no arrow nudge). Without WebGL, fall back to a dark gradient and keep all text working. Without JS, stages stack normally. Keyboard focus visible, aria-pressed on toggles, labeled sliders, no horizontal scroll at 390px. On phones the card is at most 48% of the screen height (58% when it has a mini card or is the partners pair) and scrolls inside. Before delivering: syntax-check, exercise every control and every stage in a headless browser, check mid-transition frames and phone sizes, and compare every requested change against the request. Sources on the page (all with DOI links): Janssen 2000, Mitchell 2012, Schoenfeld 2010, 2013, 2017 (dose-response), 2019 (frequency), Morton 2018, Jager 2017, Garthe 2011, Longland 2016, Mifflin 1990, Lamon 2021, Kreider 2017, Iraki 2019. Layout rules: a hidden stage must never intercept clicks; text spans inside flip-letter wrappers must not receive display:block from component CSS; punctuation touching a link or bold word stays on the same line.
